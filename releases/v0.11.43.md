## ◆ &nbsp;Installing

Import the `.mrpack` into any launcher that reads the Modrinth format — **Prism Launcher**
(*Add Instance → Import → Modrinth pack*), the **Modrinth App** (*Import → From file*), or **ATLauncher**.

The first import needs an internet connection and a few minutes: **108 of the 111 mods are downloaded from their
authors' own pages** at that point rather than being carried in the file. The first world load is also slow — the
terrain is read from image data rather than generated from noise.

**8 GB** allocated is a sensible starting point. **Distant Horizons ships at a 64-chunk radius** and is by a wide
margin the heaviest thing in the pack; turn it down first if you struggle.

## ◆ &nbsp;What the pack itself adds

Everything below is this project's own code and content, not a mod someone else wrote:

- **A layered map-driven world** — a custom generator combines terrain, biome, climate and coastline data;
  ~12.3 km across, sea at Y132.
- **40 settlements** with culture-specific architecture, furnished interiors, walls, gatehouses,
  farmland and **121 roads**; **21 harbours** with working port buildings ashore.
- **Six playable races** with traits, abilities and their own learning rates, chosen at character creation.
- **25 skill trees** levelled by doing the work, with exclusive forks so two players in a trade diverge.
  Most everyday recipes are open once their materials are available; high-end gear and specialist systems
  retain unlocks. Untrained gathering is slower and yields less, while crafting earns reduced practice XP.
- **Three schools of magic**, studied rather than bought, with their own grimoires and arts.
- **Equipment quality on every piece**, fixed to that individual item and assigned the moment it is
  obtained — nothing to roll, activate or open. Its bonuses are drawn one at a time from **82 stats**, each
  with its own grade and its own strength, about one in eight of them a flaw rather than a boon; the best
  grades can roll **18 abilities** instead of a number. Optional reforging and salvage at a smith.
- **Standing in three layers** — faction, settlement and personal — driving prices, greetings and available work.
- **335 quests**, conversation that varies by faction, mood and race, and local rumours.
- **A currency, shops with daily stock, and private trade** with eligible residents.
- **Camping storage and bedrolls** through Traveler's Backpack, with progression-aware upgrades.
- **Paid field work** with yield withholding and seed-backed replanting.
- **Crime and consequence** — stealth, theft, witnesses, the watch, fines and cells; inspectable container
  stock, protected-goods warnings and retained personal ownership through supported processing.
- **Ships bought from shipwrights**, crews who keep names, levels and loyalty, liveries, figureheads, and a sea
  that runs port-to-port trade and piracy whether or not anyone is watching.
- **Branding throughout** — title and loading screens, an unbroken musical theme, filtered chat, in-game guide,
  wallet and journal tabs, a compact seasonal survival HUD and activity-following skill XP.

## ◆ &nbsp;Unfinished systems

These remain unfinished; the roadmap distinguishes implementation from acceptance:

- **Complete relationship and romance content.**
- **The balance of the equipment economy** — the quality system is in; its prices, drop pools and endgame
  ceilings are a first draft.
- **Faction conflict, treaties and court politics.**
- **Expanded ship capture and airship gameplay.**
- The **Surazan dust-storm rhythm**.
- **World pregeneration and an update policy.**

Paid craft apprenticeships and costly discipline resets are implemented. Full quest playthroughs, cross-mod
progression acceptance, balance and multiplayer performance remain ongoing work.


## ◆ &nbsp;Changes since v0.11.31

This release brings together the changes from twelve intervening development builds.

### Progression and first days

- **Everyday crafting is open.** Ordinary tools, armour, regional metal gear, food, clothing, remedies,
  building pieces and basic stations can be crafted when their materials are available. High-end gear,
  powerful foci, advanced machinery and specialist stations retain their unlocks. Gathering remains slow
  and low-yield until trained; ordinary crafting keeps its output and earns reduced relevant-tree XP.
- **Starter recipes work without training.** The waterskin no longer incorrectly needs Alchemy. The
  Start Guide covers the inventory-grid route to a crafting table, plank and cobblestone recipes, and
  first-day water equipment. Smithing descriptions distinguish everyday crafting, trained forge practice
  and advanced unlocks.
- **XP comes from work.** Cooking trains through preparation; Alchemy through remedies, treatment and
  fresh brewing. Eating berries or prepared food and opening workstations no longer train these trades.
  Completed anvil/grindstone work, melee/ranged kills and fish/non-fish catches use their appropriate
  disciplines. Small reduced XP awards accumulate rather than rounding upward every time.
- **Calling roots and guidance.** The actual starting node is unlocked and displayed without duplicate
  point grants on reconnect or respecialisation. The Start Guide has expandable folders and first steps
  for every public discipline. Hover a tree's XP bar for its activities, or use its book link for the guide.

### Survival, camping and field work

- **Compact aligned HUD.** Armour and health sit on the left, thirst and hunger on the right. A central
  thermometer changes colour, animates warming/cooling arrows and gains frost or flames at extremes.
  A seasonal ribbon follows the calendar, and the XP strip follows the last skill practised. Open chat
  and hover for details. Air and mount controls remain contextual; enchanting XP stays separate.
  Held-item names now sit above the panel, retaining rarity colours and effects.
- **Camping backpacks.** Traveler's Backpack adds wearable storage, sleeping bags and configurable
  upgrades. A stitched pack uses everyday materials; reinforcement accepts regional metals, and advanced
  frames/upgrades follow the existing progression. Portable crafting respects normal unlocks and XP.
  Sleeping in its bedroll does not change the return point. The Start Guide explains recipes and use.
- **Paid crop shifts.** Village farmers offer daylight field work for a small fee. Existing untrained
  yield penalties remain; the farm withholds 30% of the resulting yield as crops are collected, with
  fractions carried between shifts. Leaving cannot evade the share. Permission covers crops, not
  livestock or structures. Replanting reserves real seed, and permitted harvests can very rarely yield a Bit.

### Settlements, theft and encounters

- **Consistent container property.** Sneak-use opens the full theft browser. Only ordinary junk is free;
  food, crops, ores, nuggets, bottles and other goods remain protected. Muted rust markings and tooltips
  show the protected quantity. Deposited items and supported processed outputs remain personal; mixed
  stacks return the player's share first, including purified water recovered from a settlement furnace.
- **First-open loot compatibility.** Lootr chests, barrels, shulkers and trapped chests generate and show
  the current player's stock on first inspection. Open container works while crouched and opens the
  same stock. Ownership is separate for each player's loot pool; fresh stock cannot inherit old claims.
  Normal and browser taking share theft rules, witnesses and personal-item exceptions.
- **Correct resident services.** Occupation cards, badges, clothes and map markers follow appointed
  roles. Temple keepers, innkeepers, craft teachers and special services no longer fall through to
  general-goods shops or unrelated training. Existing markers refresh on encountering their residents.
- **Wilderness follows the layout.** Natural spawns use the generated settlement lots, fields, walls,
  docks and routes instead of a painted spawn mask. Noisy 15–30-block settlement buffers and 5–10-block
  road buffers protect those edges; danger tiers and the night increase remain. These are spawn rules,
  not barriers to enemies moving in. Broader predator rosters and bandit tiers range from outlaws to
  champions; replenishment targets roughly half bandits with a slightly lower hostile budget. Passive
  animal limits are unchanged.
- **Rare enemy currency.** Eligible enemies have a very small chance to drop 1–3, 2–5 or 3–10 Bits,
  according to their threat. Luck, including Coin Sense, improves the chance; pickup banks it automatically.
- **Atmosphere.** Refined distant cloud scale and height, with a softer transition into sparse lower mist.
  Locations, inhabitants and access details remain for exploration.

### Reliability

Repairs cover inventory reward remainders, purchases with a full inventory, theft evidence, shared
processing ownership, upper sugar-cane drop attribution, Fishing predicates, equipment label
regeneration, cache cleanup and crocodile navigation tags. Generated guidance and implementation
claims have been reconciled; unchanged skill graphs, costs and unrelated mechanics are retained.

## ◆ &nbsp;Verification and remaining work

Controlled server checks cover starter crafting, progression/XP, calling starts, resident roles,
spawn geometry, equipment and service transactions, container ownership, witnessed theft, farm yield
and replanting, and backpack integration. Selected client captures cover the HUD, guide, services,
backpack and real first-open loot/browser/native-button flows. Both package flavours pass source,
dependency, archive and restriction checks; only the locked public package is published here.

This remains an alpha. Ordinary survival/co-op balance, complete story and relationship routes,
unsupported processor/automation paths, simultaneous-action XP attribution, timed refresh/process
restart acceptance and sustained performance work remain. See the
[roadmap](https://github.com/TAS-69/bathyal/wiki/Roadmap). Client and server must use matching builds.

## ◆ &nbsp;Saves

**Do not get attached to a world made with this build.** Identifiers, world format and progression change between
builds, and no migration is provided or planned during alpha.
